<p>You're stuck. You want to climb. You're considering paying for help. The two main options are <strong>coaching</strong> — paying a high-elo player to teach you — and <strong>ELO boosting</strong> — paying a high-elo player to climb on your account. They sound like alternatives but they're actually solving completely different problems, and picking the wrong one is how people end up wasting money.</p>
<p>This guide compares them honestly: cost, time, what you actually get, and a clear recommendation for which type of player should pick which.</p>
<h2 id="what-each-is">What Each One Actually Is</h2>
<h3>Coaching</h3>
<p>A high-elo player reviews your VODs, watches you live, or runs over key concepts in a voice call. You stay on your account, you play your own games, and the coach explains what you're doing wrong and what to do instead. Sessions are usually 1–2 hours, scheduled in advance, conducted via Discord screen-share or replay review. The output is knowledge — you walk away understanding wave management, jungle pathing, vision setup, or whatever the focus was.</p>
<h3>ELO Boosting</h3>
<p>A high-elo player logs into your account and plays ranked games on your behalf, climbing the ladder for you. You don't play during the order, you don't watch (unless you want to), and the output is a finished result — an account that ended a tier or two higher than where it started. You can read the full breakdown in our <a href="/blog/what-is-elo-boosting">what is ELO boosting guide</a>.</p>
<div class="callout">
<p><strong>The fundamental difference:</strong> Coaching changes <em>you</em>. Boosting changes <em>your account</em>. Once you understand that, the rest of the comparison is just figuring out which one you actually need.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="cost">Cost Breakdown — Which is Cheaper?</h2>
<p>People assume coaching is cheaper. It's not — at least not per rank gained.</p>
<h3>Coaching pricing (industry average, 2026)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Plat–Diamond coach: <strong>$15–$30 per hour</strong></li>
<li>Master+ coach: <strong>$30–$60 per hour</strong></li>
<li>Pro / Challenger coach: <strong>$80–$150 per hour</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>To climb from Silver to Gold purely through coaching, most players need 4–8 sessions plus weeks of self-grinding between them. That's roughly $80–$240 in coaching fees, with no guarantee of the rank — some players apply lessons fast, others don't.</p>
<h3>Boosting pricing</h3>
<p>An ELO boost from Silver to Gold on the Middle East server is significantly cheaper than the equivalent coaching investment, because it's a one-shot order rather than ongoing sessions. You can see exact prices for your starting and target rank on our <a href="/pricing">Pricing page</a>.</p>
<div class="callout-green">
<p><strong>💡 The honest take:</strong> If your only goal is the rank itself, boosting is almost always cheaper. If your goal is to be a better player who happens to climb, coaching is worth the higher cost-per-rank because the skill is permanent.</p>
</div>
<h2 id="time">Time Investment — Which is Faster?</h2>
<p>This is where boosting wins by a wide margin. A typical division boost on ME server completes in 1–4 days. Reaching the same rank through coaching means sitting through coach sessions, then practicing for weeks while applying what you learned, then climbing through the same MMR-and-luck system everyone else fights through. Even with a great coach, the calendar time from Silver to Gold by self-improvement is usually 4–12 weeks.</p>
<p>The asymmetry matters because most people considering coaching or boosting are stuck — and "stuck" usually means already burned out. Adding more grind on top of being already tilted is how players quit ranked entirely.</p>

<h2 id="who-boosting">Who Should Pick ELO Boosting</h2>
<p>The honest case for boosting comes down to four situations where it genuinely beats coaching:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Time-constrained players.</strong> Adults with jobs, students mid-semester, parents — anyone who can't realistically grind 4 games per day for 8 weeks. The coaching path requires that time investment; the boosting path doesn't.</li>
<li><strong>End-of-season crunch.</strong> If you have two weeks left and you want a rank reward you wouldn't otherwise hit, coaching can't get you there. A boost can.</li>
<li><strong>Broken MMR repair.</strong> When your LP gains are stuck at +13, coaching helps slowly by improving your win rate. A targeted win boost fixes it in days. Specifically for this scenario, see our <a href="/blog/what-is-mmr-league-of-legends">MMR explained guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Already-good players who hit a wall.</strong> If you're a Gold player playing at a Plat level but stuck, the missing piece often isn't a skill gap — it's matchmaking variance. A boost gets you to where your skill already is.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="both">Why Some Players Use Both</h2>
<p>The smart play, when budget allows, is using them together. The combination that works best:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Boost first.</strong> Get to your target rank quickly. This solves the immediate frustration and gives you matches at a level that better matches your aspirational play, not your tilted play.</li>
<li><strong>Coach second.</strong> Now that you're at the rank you wanted, take 2–3 coaching sessions to learn what you need to <em>stay</em> there. The coaching investment is now protecting a rank you actually have, not paying to slowly earn one.</li>
</ol>
<p>This sequence works because most players who climb purely by boosting drop back down within a season. Most players who climb purely by coaching never reach their ceiling because they tilt out of grinding. Combining the two solves both failure modes.</p>
