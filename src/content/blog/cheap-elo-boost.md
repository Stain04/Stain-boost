<p>Everyone wants a cheap ELO boost — and there's nothing wrong with that. But in boosting, the price isn't just a number: it tells you how the boost will be played. This guide explains why some boosts are suspiciously cheap, what you risk with them, and the honest ways to pay less for a boost that keeps your account safe.</p>

<h2 id="why-cheap">Why Some Boosts Are So Cheap</h2>
<p>A boost costs a skilled player's time — many games of it. When a price is far below what others charge, the saving usually comes from one of these:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Large booster pools paid per game:</strong> the order goes to whoever accepts the lowest rate, often a different player each session.</li>
<li><strong>Rushed play:</strong> many games back to back, sometimes on several accounts at once, with no care for how the account looks afterwards.</li>
<li><strong>Scripts or bots:</strong> the fastest way to cut costs, and the fastest way to get an account banned.</li>
<li><strong>Skipped protection:</strong> no offline mode, no VPN — so your account suddenly logs in from another country, in front of your whole friends list.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="real-cost">The Real Cost of a Too-Cheap Boost</h2>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>A suspended or banned account</strong> costs far more than the money you saved — especially if you've bought skins over the years.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>Low LP gains after the boost:</strong> a booster who loses many games on the way up leaves your MMR low, so your own wins give less LP afterwards. <a href="/blog/why-lp-gains-are-low">Why LP gains drop</a> explains it.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>No refund when it goes wrong:</strong> rock-bottom services rarely have a refund policy worth the name.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="pay-less">7 Honest Ways to Pay Less for a Safe Boost</h2>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 01</div>
<h3>Choose Solo Instead of Duo</h3>
<p>Solo boosts (the booster plays on your account) cost less than duo boosts, because only one player is involved. Silver IV to Gold IV, for example, costs $40 solo and $56 duo.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 02</div>
<h3>Use Net Wins for Short Climbs</h3>
<p>If you're close to the next division, a handful of wins can cost less than a division boost. Net wins start at $1.50 per win in Iron, $2.50 in Silver and $3.50 in Gold.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 03</div>
<h3>Order Wins in Fives</h3>
<p>With pay for 5, get 6, every 5 wins you pay for include 1 free. Ten paid wins become 12 — the most value per dollar.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 04</div>
<h3>Use a Friend's Referral Code</h3>
<p>A referral code takes <strong>20% off your first order</strong>. If a friend has ordered before, ask them for their code — they also earn credit for the next one.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 05</div>
<h3>Enter Your Current LP</h3>
<p>If you're halfway through a division, you only pay for the part that's left. Set your current LP in the configurator and the first division is discounted.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 06</div>
<h3>Pay Only for the Divisions You Need</h3>
<p>Every division is priced on its own, so you never pay for a full tier you don't need. Silver II to Gold IV is just two Silver divisions — $20 solo. And low LP gains don't cost extra: the price per division is the same either way.</p>
</div>

<div class="tip-card">
<div class="tip-number">Way 07</div>
<h3>Skip Extras You Don't Need</h3>
<p>Priority start and the bonus win are optional. If you're not in a hurry, leave them off.</p>
</div>
