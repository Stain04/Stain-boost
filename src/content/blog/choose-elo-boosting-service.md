<p>Search for "ELO boost" and you'll find dozens of sites that all look the same: big discounts, five-star reviews and promises of a fast, safe climb. Some are run by skilled, careful players. Others pass your account to whoever is cheapest that day. Because you're trusting a stranger with your account, <strong>choosing the right service matters more than the price</strong>.</p>
<p>This guide lists the red flags that should make you close the tab, and the signs of a boosting service you can trust.</p>

<h2 id="red-flags">9 Red Flags of an Unsafe Boosting Service</h2>

<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>1. Reviews you can't verify.</strong> Hundreds of perfect reviews with no names, no ranks and no link to a real order are easy to fake. Look for reviews that come from completed orders, ideally with the rank achieved.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>2. "100% safe" or "zero ban risk" promises.</strong> Boosting is against Riot's Terms of Service, so no one can honestly promise zero risk. A service that does is either misinformed or not telling you the truth — neither is a good sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>3. You never know who plays your account.</strong> Many sites hand orders to a pool of boosters. Your account may be played by several people with different skills and habits. Ask who will play, and whether you can see their profile.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>4. Prices far below everyone else's.</strong> A boost has a real cost in time. When a price is far below the market, the difference usually comes from somewhere: rushed games, account sharing or scripts. See <a href="/blog/cheap-elo-boost">what a cheap boost really gets you</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>5. No word on how the account is protected.</strong> A careful booster plays in offline mode and uses a VPN in your region. If a site doesn't mention any of this, assume it isn't done.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>6. They ask for your email password.</strong> A booster needs your League login to play solo — never the password to your email. Anyone asking for it is a risk to every account you own.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>7. No clear refund policy.</strong> You should know before paying what happens if the boost doesn't start or you change your mind. "Contact support" isn't a policy.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>8. No way to follow progress.</strong> If you can't see which games were played and how your LP is moving, you have no way of knowing what's happening on your account.</p>
</div>
<div class="mistake-card">
<p><strong>9. Hidden fees at checkout.</strong> A price that grows at the last step — service fees, "express" charges you didn't choose — tells you how the rest of the order will go.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="green-flags">What a Trustworthy Boosting Service Looks Like</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>A booster you can look up:</strong> a real in-game name with a visible rank history on op.gg.</li>
<li><strong>Honest about risk:</strong> it explains what it does to keep your account safe, without pretending risk doesn't exist.</li>
<li><strong>Transparent pricing:</strong> you see the full price, including extras, before you pay.</li>
<li><strong>A real refund policy</strong> written on the site.</li>
<li><strong>Live progress:</strong> a tracker or regular updates with every game played.</li>
<li><strong>Direct contact</strong> with the person playing, not just a ticket system.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="protect">How to Protect Your Account During Any Boost</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Use a unique password</strong> for your League account — never one you use anywhere else.</li>
<li><strong>Never share your email login</strong>, only your League login, and only for solo boosts.</li>
<li><strong>Change your password</strong> when the boost is complete.</li>
<li><strong>Choose duo</strong> if you'd rather not share your login at all — you keep full control of your account.</li>
<li><strong>Don't play on the account</strong> while the booster is in a game, and agree on times in advance.</li>
</ol>

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>A quick test before you pay:</strong> send the service a question — "who will play my account?" or "what happens if you can't finish?" — and see how fast and how clearly they answer. A good service answers plainly. A vague or pushy reply is a red flag on its own.</p>
</div>
